A feature release. hacsf2 now converts pieces that wait for values from the game, such as the rhythm pieces of Super Mario Party Jamboree and Super Mario Party, and Jamboree's star melody. Sounds of two Mario Party games pick their variants as in the game. Conversions of the WarioWare games play at the levels in their sound sheets. The reverbs that three games put on effect bus B now reach the synth's reverb. Convert an archive again to get the new files.

Requires 64-bit Windows 10 or later. No additional runtime DLLs are required. Drop one or more `.bfsar` files onto `hacsf2.exe`. You can also use a terminal. `hacsf2 --help` lists the options. Output goes in a folder beside each archive.

For macOS on Apple Silicon, follow the build instructions in the README. macOS builds include `Hacsf2.app`. Drop archives onto the app in Finder to convert them.

## New

- **Rhythm pieces.** The rhythm mode of Super Mario Party Jamboree and the Sound Stage of Super Mario Party play pieces that wait until the game sets their tempo and number. hacsf2 used to convert those pieces to silence. hacsf2 now recognizes the sub-archives of both modes and converts each piece with the tempo and number that the game sets. `--var` overrides them. In the game, a controller sequence plays beside each piece. The controller adds a kick drum on each beat of many of the pieces. hacsf2 converts each piece together with the controller into one MIDI file. The two exchange the game's variables as they do in the game. The controller's tracks come after the tracks of the piece. The SoundFont contains the samples of both. A piece keeps its loop where the controller repeats with it. When hacsf2 can't find the controller's archive, it warns and converts the pieces without the controller. The README says where hacsf2 looks.
- **Jamboree's star melody.** The star melody follows the beat and the chords of the board's music. The game plays it over one of the streams of the boards. hacsf2 used to convert the melody to silence. hacsf2 now converts it once for each stream whose file and jump setting are beside the archive. Each conversion is named after the melody and the stream. The conversion follows one pass through the stream, then its loop once more. The stream itself isn't part of the conversion. The README says which of the game's files to put beside the archive.
- **Variants that the game's code picks.** In Super Mario Party Jamboree and Mario Party Superstars, a sound can pick one of its variants through a user procedure. That's code of the game that draws a number and passes it to the sequence. Such a sound used to play a note past its variants, or nothing. hacsf2 now recognizes every sound archive of the two games and runs their procedures as the game's code does. Each sequence starts from the state that the game's code sets up for the procedures as the game starts. One of Jamboree's procedures reads a table from the game's files. The README says where hacsf2 looks for it.
- **Sounds that wait for the game's state.** Some sound effects play only once the game has written one of their variables as it starts them. Others play only on some draws of the game's random numbers. Such an effect used to convert to silence. hacsf2 now starts it in a state that the game can set up. For Super Mario 3D World + Bowser's Fury and some archives of Super Mario Party Jamboree, hacsf2 contains the values that the game's code writes. It tries each of them until the effect plays a note above volume 0. An effect that draws a number and plays nothing from the default seed converts from a later start. hacsf2 steps the random generator once a frame, as the game does, for 1 to 400 frames. The conversion uses the first start that plays such a note. A `--var` value replaces the value for its variable. `--seed` turns the search of later starts off.
- **Levels from the sound sheets.** WarioWare: Get It Together! and WarioWare: Move It! run on Bezel Engine. Such a game lists a level for each sound in its sound sheets, outside the sound archive. hacsf2 now reads the sheets and plays each sequence at its level. hacsf2 looks for a `Data/SheetBin` folder near the archive. The new option `--sheets DIR` names the folder when it's elsewhere. Without sheets, each sequence still plays at the archive's level.
- **Layered pieces in WarioWare: Get It Together!** Some of the game's microgames turn tracks of their music up and down as the microgame goes on. hacsf2 recognizes the game's sound archive. Each of those pieces now converts in the state that the game starts it in. Every track used to play. Each later state gets a further conversion. That conversion is named after the piece and the state. `--list` shows them. A track that a state turns down plays at CC11 0 from the start. Its notes stay in the MIDI file.
- **Reverbs on effect bus B.** Each microgame of WarioWare: Get It Together! puts a reverb on effect bus B as it starts. WarioWare: Move It! keeps a reverb there for every sequence. Clubhouse Games: 51 Worldwide Classics puts one there in its piano and bowling games. hacsf2 used to play a send to such a bus dry through CC11. A send to one of these reverbs now feeds CC91, the synth's reverb, together with effect send A. CC11 keeps only the dry part of the reverb. A sequence of a microgame converts with the reverb of that microgame. The other sequences of the game that send to bus B convert with bus B empty. Each of them also gets a conversion with each reverb. Such a conversion is named after the sequence and the reverb. Move It! also puts a delay on bus A. In the conversions of Move It!, CC91 contains only the sends to bus B.

## Fixes

- **Pitch limits.** nn::atk plays a voice at 1/16 to 16 times the rate of its wave. In the game, a note that asks for a pitch past a limit plays at the limit. A note whose key starts past a limit and whose pitch holds still now gets zones and regions that play at the limit too. Such a note used to play at the pitch of its key.
- **SoundFonts past the format's limits.** A SoundFont contains at most 65,535 zones, generators and modulators. A sequence with many presets of large instruments can pass that limit. hacsf2 used to stop with "SoundFont too large" for such a sequence. The SoundFont now contains only the zones that the notes of the sequence pick. hacsf2 prints a warning. A synth picks zones by a note's key and velocity as the note starts. The zones left out never sound. The SFZ files keep every zone.
- **The end of a finished sequence.** A track can wait, or change a controller, long after the last sound. A sequence that finished used to end at its last change. When that change comes more than 10 s after the last sound has died away, the MIDI file now ends where that sound dies away.

## Other changes

- **The README on sfizz.** The list of sfizz 1.2.3's quirks has a new entry. sfizz makes each decay and release last at least 6 ms. The SFZ documentation sets no such floor.
- **The libraries.** hacsf2 now builds on seqlib 1.3.0, xsflib 1.8.0 and ntdlib 3.2.0.

## Known limitations

As in 1.0.5, apart from the changes above. A sound that picks a variant plays the same variant in every conversion. In the game, the variant changes from play to play. The star melody converts without its stream. The game can play a piece through other records of its sound sheets, at other levels. hacsf2 uses the record named after the sound. Where the game varies a level at random, hacsf2 uses the middle of the range. A note past a pitch limit that a bend, a sweep or an LFO moves still plays past the limit. See "Differences from the game" in the README for the full list.

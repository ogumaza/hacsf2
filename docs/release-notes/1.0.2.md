A release that comes closer to the game's tone. The Switch's audio renderer darkens a voice that reads its wave faster than 48,000 samples a second. Such notes now play darker in the SoundFonts and SFZ files too. Convert an archive again to get the corrected files.

Requires 64-bit Windows 10 or later. No additional runtime DLLs are required. Drop one or more `.bfsar` files onto `hacsf2.exe`. You can also use a terminal. `hacsf2 --help` lists the options. Output goes in a folder beside each archive.

For macOS on Apple Silicon, follow the build instructions in the README. macOS builds include `Hacsf2.app`. Drop archives onto the app in Finder to convert them.

## Fixes

- **The renderer's resampling.** The audio renderer resamples each voice to 48 kHz with one of three filters. A voice that reads its wave faster than 48,000 samples a second loses up to about 2 dB at 12 kHz and 8 dB at 24 kHz. SoundFont zones and SFZ regions that play their waves that fast now have a low-pass whose cutoff follows the key. Zones split where a key reads its wave at 48,000 and 64,000 samples a second. The renderer changes filters at those rates. Such notes used to play brighter than in the game.
- **Reports.** The SoundFont leaves the new low-pass out of a preset whose filter changes during notes. hacsf2 reports the notes of such a preset that read their waves faster than 48,000 samples a second. The SFZ files keep the low-pass.

## Other changes

- **FluidSynth's level.** The README explains a difference in FluidSynth's level. FluidSynth plays every note 1.5 dB louder than the SoundFont specifies. Multiplying FluidSynth's gain by 0.84 gives the specified level.

## Known limitations

As in 1.0.1, apart from the fixes above. A note keeps the low-pass of its key. A bend, sweep or LFO that takes the note past 48,000 or 64,000 samples a second doesn't change it. See "Differences from the game" in the README for the full list.
